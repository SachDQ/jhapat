# Jhapat (झपट)

Sachin's web app for a Nepali party game. Items such as paani, phool, chiya, paisa, chithi and maya sit on a table. The player drops into a random song 7–10 s before one of those words is sung, and the crowd grabs and referees in real life. It is a music player, not a scoring app: no scores, no timers, no rules engine.

Live at https://sachdq.github.io/jhapat/ (GitHub Pages, `main` branch, repo root). Pushing to `main` deploys in about a minute. Pages sends `max-age=600`, so add `?v=<commit>` to the URL to see a new version straight away. Push over SSH (`git@github.com:SachDQ/jhapat.git`); there's no `gh` CLI or HTTPS credential on Sachin's Mac.

Versions keep Sachin and Claude in step: run `npm run bump` before each push that changes a page (not for docs-only commits). It raises `VERSION` in both pages and `version.json` together (the tests check they match). The player shows it at the bottom, the studio next to the logo. A page that finds a newer `version.json` on the site shows a "New version · Reload" button, which reloads with `?v=` past the cache. When telling Sachin what to test, name the version he should see.

## Layout
- `index.html`: the party player, a single file with no build step. It plays YouTube through the IFrame API in two stacked players: the front one plays, the back one loads the next drop muted. iPhone, iPad and Safari use one player, because they only give sound to a video the person has tapped. Flow: pick a playlist → Play screen shows only that playlist's items with counts → Start. Since v30 everything comes in one call, `rpc/catalog`: every public song once with its uploads and tags, and each official playlist as a list of song ids (465 KB, 136 KB gzipped, about 1.4 s, on 14 Nov). It's stored under its own key, `jhapat.cat`, and the songs are rebuilt from it on each load, so the save (`jhapat.v1`, about 5 KB) stays quick to write and every playlist works offline once the catalog has loaded; a phone with no room for it keeps its songs in the save as before. The picker lists All songs (only when it adds songs to the biggest playlist; its covers are each playlist's first song), the official playlists, the YouTube playlists made on this phone, and Use a YouTube playlist; card item counts are songs with a drop point for the item. In `jhapat.v1`: `S.playlist`, `S.chosen`, `S.tables` (items turned on per playlist; only real toggles are stored), `S.mine` (YouTube playlists), `S.asked` (videos this phone asked for), `S.reported` and `S.outbox` (reports, and ones waiting for signal), `S.dropAt`, `S.played`. A playlist opened for the first time also turns on any item every one of its songs has. Item balance (Settings, 0–10, default 5, `S.settings.balance`): the next drop picks an item first, then one of its songs not played yet. Each item's odds are `balance/10 ÷ items + (1 − balance/10) × its share of the songs`, so at 5 half the drops go round the items in turn and half follow the songs: Maya, in most songs, still gets the most without crowding out the rest. Strict even turns (v23–v25, now balance 10) kept repeating a rare item's two or three songs; 60 simulated drops of Maya + Chithi on Party Mix played one Chithi song 5 times, against 2–3 at balance 5. An item whose songs have all played counts a quarter until the list starts again, a song never comes back within the last 10 drops (half the list in a short one), and Settings shows the next drop's odds. Drop points: the run-up is random in 7–10 s and never reaches back past an earlier table word (1.2 s kept after it, since a tag marks where the word starts), so the room never hears another table word first. A line sung more than once counts once (`[item, t, rep]` in the bundle; a tag without a group stands alone): each line's item word drops in at the first time the line is sung with room for the full 7 s run-up, else the first with at least 4.5 s (the run-up then takes what there is). So the same word twice close together drops at the first; of two different table words close together the earlier gets the drop, and the later drops at its line's next roomy repeat if it has one. Every line with room is a drop point, so a song has several, near the end too: each time a song comes back for an item it drops in at that item's next drop point, the earliest first (`S.dropAt`, per song and item). The 7 s and 4.5 s don't follow the Drop-in range, so drop points always match the studio's red dots. An item is only offered from songs where it has a drop point, its chip counts those songs (the hover gives its drop points), and the pool line counts the songs the table can drop into. Use a YouTube playlist (v31): a hidden YouTube player reads a pasted playlist's video IDs (`cuePlaylist`, then `getPlaylist()`: the first 200, public or unlisted, no key, under a second) and YouTube's oEmbed gives each title (8 at a time: 40 videos in 1.4 s). A video matches a Jhapat upload first, then by name, with names compared as they sound (vowel runs count as one, ph = f, sh = s, an h after a consonant is dropped, w = v = b): the song's title as whole words in the video's title, plus its artist in the title or channel (also with spaces taken out: Rajanraj = Rajan Raj), or a long title (14+ letters, 3+ words) that is a whole piece of the video's title. Short titles need the artist: Timi Ra Ma by another singer once matched. Three public Nepali playlists (223 videos) gave 65 matches, all right on inspection. Matched songs play from Jhapat's own timed upload; the rest are listed with covers, and Ask for these sends them to `request_songs`. Songs added to Jhapat later join the playlist by themselves when the catalog changes. Share copies `https://sachdq.github.io/jhapat/#yt=<list id>`, which reads YouTube again on whatever phone opens it. Something off? (v32), under Next song, sends what was wrong (word came too soon, came late, wrong word, not sung, video won't play, or a note) with the song, upload, word, its time, run-up and version to `report_drop`; without signal it waits in `S.outbox` for the next visit that reaches the database. The 16 songs built into `seed()` are only a fallback for phones that have never reached the database. Kept short on purpose: the video box stays folded until the first song plays, Start stands alone until then, a long item list shows its first 8 (plus any that are on or were tapped) and "+N more", run-up and video options sit in one folded Settings card, and playlist cards fan out their first songs' covers (`playlist_cards.thumbs`, YouTube's `mqdefault.jpg`).
- The look (v33, "Night Festival", from the plan at https://claude.ai/artifact/AgBEhQuFk8yQv4AMft9qU2): the player is night-only. Its colours are raat `#0F0B1E` behind everything, saanjh `#1D1636` cards, marigold `#FFA41B` for anything pressed (items that are on are marigold with dark text), diyo flame `#FFD25E` for glow and focus, tiger orange `#F26B1D` where things move, and moonlight `#FFF4E0` text. It has string lights along the top that twinkle, the marigold garland under the header, and the tiger logo beside "Jhapat" and a झपट in tiger stripes. Sachin asked for lots of slot-machine fun, so each drop spins a reel of song titles that lands on the real one (the real title stays in the page for screen readers and tests), swipes three glowing claw slashes over the video, jolts the screen, makes the logo leap and buzzes Android phones. Tiger stripes prowl along the top of Now playing while the run-up plays. Show the word pops the word with a burst of its item's emoji, an item going on springs with sparks, the song count flips when it changes, Start breathes and shakes now and then, and a drops-tonight counter (🔥, `S.night`, starts again after 4 quiet hours) cheers at 5, 10, 25, 50 and 100. Everything is CSS transforms and opacity under a second, never blocks a tap or delays the music, and switches off with the phone's reduce-motion setting.
- `img/`: `jhapat-mark.webp` (the leaping tiger reaching for a diyo, 245×128, 10 KB, used by both pages) and `icon-32/180/192/512.png` (a tiger face under a diyo flame on night indigo: tab icon, iPhone home screen, and `manifest.webmanifest` for Android). Sachin had them made with GPT Image 2.6 "Sunburst" on kie.ai's playground (14 Nov; he signs in himself; about 17 credits, $0.08, an image at 1K xhigh). The playground keeps PNG output for Sunburst even when SVG is asked for; the mark used `background: transparent`, the icon `opaque`. The 1536 px and 1024 px originals are in `backups/logo-2026-11-14/` (kept out of git). Prompts asked for a minimal flat vector style in the palette's colours, no text, readable at 32–40 px.
- `studio/index.html`: the tagging studio, for editors only. It uses supabase-js 2.45.4 (UMD; verified live with the publishable key) and signs in by email link. It keeps its light working colours and has the tiger logo, the striped झपट and the icons since v33. It searches LRCLIB for timed lyrics, folds repeated lines together, highlights item words, and supports Hear/Tap/nudge per word with the timing copied to every repeat. A tap is refused unless it lands from 1 s before a repeat's line to 0.5 s after the next line starts. It also handles upload length checks (within 2 s of the lyrics → synced) and sync, items and playlists. Untimed lyrics get **Time the lines**: play the upload and tap as each line starts (Again for a line sung more often than it's written, Skip for one that isn't sung, Undo); the taps become timed lines, every item word is auto-tagged from them, repeated lines group themselves, the upload counts as synced and a draft goes live as auto. A draft whose timed lyrics come from another recording offers **Time the lines again** on its own upload: the old estimates make way for new ones, tags timed by hand stay, and other uploads need a new sync. Item-by-item tap-along stays for songs with no lyrics at all (folded away when there are words to time). Item words are coloured by state (dashed = not tagged, light gold = estimated, gold = timed or checked by ear); "Auto-tag them" estimates every untagged one, and Items › "Auto-tag all songs" does it everywhere after a new item or spelling. **Check tags** plays only the drop points, from 3 s before with a countdown, one stop per line (a chorus counts once). Drop points follow the player's rule, worked out for every mix of the song's items on the table (`dropSet`), so the red dots in the lyrics are exactly the words the game can drop into; a dot that only applies with another item out says so in its hover text ("when Maya is out too"). Right marks it checked, Earlier/Later moves every repeat. Every item word stays tagged, so the player knows where each word is and never starts a run-up across one, but only drop points are ever dropped into, so only they need hearing. The Tags card counts words and drop points per item, the song list and item tiles count drop points, and each song shows when it was added, changed, last tagged by hand and last checked by ear (`songs.tagged_at` and `checked_at`, kept by statement triggers on tags; the song list sorts by them). **Requests** (`#requests`, v31) lists songs people asked for from their YouTube playlists, most asked first, flagged "Maybe already in Jhapat" by the player's name rule; picked ones go into Add many as `Title - Artist | link` lines (the channel's own name dropped from the title, " - Topic" from the channel), and adding a song closes its request. **Reports** (`#reports`, v32) lists drops reported from the player, newest first; Open plays the reported word from 3 s before in Check tags, on the upload that played, with Mark the report fixed. Both tabs show a count of open ones. **Add many** (`#import`) takes `Title - Artist | YouTube link | m:ss` lines, measures each upload in a muted off-screen player, picks LRCLIB's version of the same length, auto-tags it and sets status `auto`; the rest come in as drafts. A line can end with `| lrclib:12345` to use that exact LRCLIB version (fetched by id, one request, no search). LRCLIB allows about 45 requests a minute from a browser and 90 from a script with its own User-Agent, per IP, so the studio spaces requests 1.4 s apart and a "too many requests" answer waits for its Retry-After and asks again; a row it never answers is marked and left out of Add, and "Find again" looks up only those rows. Don't run other LRCLIB scripts from the same computer while Add many is working.
  The song list loads in pages of 1,000 (Supabase's limit per request), so it keeps working past 1,000 songs. Lists stay short: Songs has covers, item counts per song, a search, status filters (Auto, Tagged, Checked, Drafts, Needs work), a playlist filter and a sort. Items are tiles (first 12, then "Show all") with one editor that opens above them; New item and Auto-tag all songs sit at the top. Playlists are folded cards with covers; open one to rename, reorder, take songs out (✕) or add them by typing. On a song, the Uploads and Playlists cards fold to a one-line summary (Uploads folds itself while its first upload is synced), and "Only lines with item words" hides the rest of the lyrics. Folds, the song sort and that switch are remembered in localStorage (`jhapat.studio`); filters reset on reload.
- `db/schema.sql`: tables, row-level security, `catalog()` (v30; the player's only call since then), `requests` and `reports` with `request_songs(vids)` and `report_drop(r)` (v31–v32: anyone adds through those functions, which check what they get; only editors read or change the tables), `playlist_bundle(pid)` and the `playlist_cards` view (both kept for pages cached before v30) (its `thumbs` column, the first four songs' uploads, was added on 13 Nov), and the tag triggers that date songs (`tagged_at`, `checked_at`, added on 14 Nov with a backfill from the hand-timed and checked tags' creation times). `db/seed.sql` holds the 16 starter songs. Both were run once in the Supabase SQL editor; edit them, then re-run the changed parts there.

## Data rules
- Tag times are on the lyrics' timeline. Each upload (`videos.offset_s`) stores how far it runs behind the lyrics, so video time = lyric time + offset. Never re-tag because an upload changed; fix that upload's offset.
- `songs.status`: draft (hidden) → auto (tagged automatically from lyrics the same length as the upload; public) → tagged (someone tagged or timed by hand) → checked. Tagging by hand moves draft or auto to tagged.
- `tags.rep` is the first line of the tag's repeated-line group (null for tap-along tags); the studio writes it with `line` and `word`.
- Item spellings (`items.words`) match at the start of a lyric word, in Devanagari and roman letters. A spelling starting with `-` rules words out for that item (`-दामी`, `-full`, `-रातो`). Don't add short roman spellings that are common words: roman `pani` was dropped from Paani because it's usually पनि (also). `items.emoji` shows on chips; `items.note` is the prop to put out.
- Lyrics live in the database and are fetched from LRCLIB at runtime. Don't copy lyrics into the repo, tests or docs. Test fixtures use invented lines.

## Adding songs (the process)
1. Sachin sends a list of songs. Claude finds an upload for each in his Chrome (YouTube search results carry each video's length): prefer "Artist - Topic" or official audio, then the official video. Check embedding with `https://www.youtube.com/oembed?url=…` (401 = blocked).
2. Paste `Title - Artist | https://youtu.be/ID | m:ss` lines into studio › Songs › Add many, pick a playlist, Find, check the picks (LRCLIB's version with the same length is chosen), Add.
3. Same length → live as `auto`, every item word tagged. Otherwise a draft: sync by ear, or tap along when LRCLIB has no timed lyrics.
4. Polish with Check tags on the phone or laptop.

Many songs at once (how 610 were added on 13 Nov 2026, after 172 earlier that day, and 200 more on 14 Nov), from the terminal plus Add many:
- Fastest, LRCLIB first: search LRCLIB for each artist already in the game (`/api/search?q=<artist>` returns up to 20 versions with their timed lyrics in one request; for artists with more, add a common word such as maya, timi or mero), keep the Nepali ones with item words, then find each one's Topic upload of the same length on YouTube Music, which has no rate limit. 261 artists took about 720 LRCLIB requests and gave 543 songs that could go live. Rank them by YouTube Music's play count (the "11M plays" column in its song search) and add the popular ones; 361 had at least 100,000 plays.
- The round after (14 Nov: 200 songs for 386 LRCLIB requests) reused what earlier rounds had already checked: their finds under 100,000 plays, and their finds with no same-length Topic upload, looked up again with more queries and then YouTube Music's videos filter (`params: EgWKAQIQAWoKEAkQBRAKEAMQBA==`) for an official video within 1.5 s of the lyrics (87 found). New LRCLIB searches went only to the 66 artists the game had gained, plus artist + a common word (prem, jindagi, aankha, sapana, yaad, pirati, hajur, mayalu) for the 40 with the most songs. Of 340 that could go live, the 200 most played went in (at least 25,000 plays), after dropping a second version of the same song (keep the Topic upload), titles that were only the artist's name, tracks over 15 minutes, and other spellings of songs already in the game (compare titles with every vowel run counted as one sound, so Yahi and Yehi Ho Ta Maya match).
- Starting from playlists works too but wastes LRCLIB requests: most playlist songs aren't on LRCLIB with timed lyrics (of 1,251 found in 25 YouTube playlists, 299 could go live).
- Track lists: Spotify's embed page `https://open.spotify.com/embed/playlist/<id>` holds the first 100 tracks with lengths in its `__NEXT_DATA__` JSON (the web player, signed in, has the rest). YouTube Music playlists come from its web API (`music.youtube.com/youtubei/v1/browse`, client `WEB_REMIX`); its search with the "songs" filter (`params: EgWKAQIIAWoKEAkQBRAKEAMQBA==`) returns the Topic uploads with their lengths, which match Spotify's to the second. Plain YouTube search buries them.
- For each song: the Topic upload of the same length (±2 s), LRCLIB timed lyrics of that length whose artist shares a name with the song's (a cover's timing would be off), lyrics that are actually Nepali, oEmbed 200, and at least one item word. For the language, count words only Nepali uses (छ, मेरो, तिमी, timi, mero, chha…) against words only Hindi uses (है, मैं, तेरा, hai, mera, tujhe…): Devanagari alone lets Hindi through, and a Turkish song once sat in a "Nepali" playlist. Rap is left out for now: a list of rappers plus more than 2 words a second in the timed lyrics. So are kids' rhymes and dance-challenge videos. Only those go in, so every one lands as `auto`.
- Give each line its `| lrclib:<id>` so Add many takes that version instead of searching. Shorten titles that contain " - " (it splits Title - Artist).
- LRCLIB rate-limits by IP: keep scripts under about 75 requests a minute with an own User-Agent, honour Retry-After, and run one copy at a time (two copies once doubled the load). Pause a script while Add many is finding (`kill -STOP <pid>`, then `kill -CONT <pid>` once it's adding): they share the IP's allowance. Its database dump would avoid the limit but is 49 GB compressed (Oct 2026), more than the Mac's free space. LRCGET is a desktop app for tagging local music files through the same API, so it doesn't help. Add many takes about 1.4 s a song to find and 1.7 s to add; 100 at a time is fine.
