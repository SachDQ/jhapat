# Jhapat (झपट)

Sachin's web app for a Nepali party game. Items such as paani, phool, chiya, paisa, chithi and maya sit on a table. The player drops into a random song 7–10 s before one of those words is sung, and the crowd grabs and referees in real life. It is a music player, not a scoring app: no scores, no timers, no rules engine.

Live at https://jhapat.live/ (GitHub Pages, `main` branch, repo root). Sachin bought the domain at GoDaddy on 14 Nov. Its DNS has four A records for @ (185.199.108.153 to 185.199.111.153, GitHub Pages) and www as a CNAME to sachdq.github.io, and the repo's `CNAME` file names it. The old https://sachdq.github.io/jhapat/ redirects there. A phone keeps its save (table, YouTube playlists, drop count) per address, so its first visit to jhapat.live starts fresh. Pushing to `main` deploys in about a minute. Pages sends `max-age=600`, so add `?v=<commit>` to the URL to see a new version straight away. Push over SSH (`git@github.com:SachDQ/jhapat.git`); there's no `gh` CLI or HTTPS credential on Sachin's Mac.

Versions keep Sachin and Claude in step: run `npm run bump` before each push that changes a page (not for docs-only commits). It raises `VERSION` in both pages and `version.json` together (the tests check they match). The player shows it at the bottom, the studio next to the logo. A page that finds a newer `version.json` on the site shows a "New version · Reload" button, which reloads with `?v=` past the cache. When telling Sachin what to test, name the version he should see.

## Layout
- `index.html`: the party player, a single file with no build step. It plays YouTube through the IFrame API in two stacked players: the front one plays, the back one loads the next drop muted. iPhone, iPad and Safari use one player, because they only give sound to a video the person has tapped. Flow: pick a playlist → Play screen shows only that playlist's items with counts → Start. Since v30 everything comes in one call, `rpc/catalog`: every public song once with its uploads and tags, and each official playlist as a list of song ids (465 KB, 136 KB gzipped, about 1.4 s, on 14 Nov). It's stored under its own key, `jhapat.cat`, and the songs are rebuilt from it on each load, so the save (`jhapat.v1`, about 5 KB) stays quick to write and every playlist works offline once the catalog has loaded; a phone with no room for it keeps its songs in the save as before. The picker lists All songs (only when it adds songs to the biggest playlist; its covers are each playlist's first song), the official playlists, the YouTube playlists made on this phone, and Use a YouTube playlist; card item counts are songs with a drop point for the item. In `jhapat.v1`: `S.playlist`, `S.chosen`, `S.tables` (items turned on per playlist; only real toggles are stored), `S.mine` (YouTube playlists), `S.asked` (videos this phone asked for), `S.reported` and `S.outbox` (reports, and ones waiting for signal), `S.dropAt`, `S.played`, `S.donePts`. A playlist opened for the first time also turns on any item every one of its songs has. Item balance (Settings, 0–10, default 5, `S.settings.balance`): the next drop picks an item first (never the item that just dropped, while another is on), then one of its songs with a line for it that hasn't dropped yet, songs not heard in the last 10 drops first. Each item's odds are `balance/10 ÷ items + (1 − balance/10) × its share of the songs`, so at 5 half the drops go round the items in turn and half follow the songs: Maya, in most songs, still gets the most without crowding out the rest. Strict even turns (v23–v25, now balance 10) kept repeating a rare item's two or three songs; 60 simulated drops of Maya + Chithi on Party Mix played one Chithi song 5 times, against 2–3 at balance 5. Songs come back for their other lines (Sachin, 14 Nov: the worry is the same item twice in a row, not the same song). Every line for the table drops once a round, full run-ups first (`S.donePts`, keyed by song, item and line). A song never plays twice in a row (not within the last 2 drops), and that rule gives way before an item would repeat. An item whose lines have all dropped counts a quarter until the round starts again, and Settings shows the next drop's odds. Drop points: the run-up is random in 7–10 s and never reaches back past an earlier table word (1.2 s kept after it, since a tag marks where the word starts), so the room never hears another table word first. A line sung more than once counts once (`[item, t, rep]` in the bundle; a tag without a group stands alone): each line's item word drops in at the first time the line is sung with room for the full 7 s run-up, else the first with at least 4.5 s (the run-up then takes what there is). So the same word twice close together drops at the first; of two different table words close together the earlier gets the drop, and the later drops at its line's next roomy repeat if it has one. Every line with room is a drop point, so a song has several, near the end too: each time a song comes back for an item it drops in at that item's next drop point, the earliest first (`S.dropAt`, per song and item). The 7 s and 4.5 s don't follow the Drop-in range, so drop points always match the studio's red dots. An item is only offered from songs where it has a drop point, its chip counts those songs (the hover gives its drop points), and the pool line counts the songs the table can drop into and the drops left this round. Use a YouTube playlist (v31): a hidden YouTube player reads a pasted playlist's video IDs (`cuePlaylist`, then `getPlaylist()`: the first 200, public or unlisted, no key, under a second) and YouTube's oEmbed gives each title (8 at a time: 40 videos in 1.4 s). A video matches a Jhapat upload first, then by name, with names compared as they sound (vowel runs count as one, ph = f, sh = s, an h after a consonant is dropped, w = v = b): the song's title as whole words in the video's title, plus its artist in the title or channel (also with spaces taken out: Rajanraj = Rajan Raj), or a long title (14+ letters, 3+ words) that is a whole piece of the video's title. Short titles need the artist: Timi Ra Ma by another singer once matched. Three public Nepali playlists (223 videos) gave 65 matches, all right on inspection. Matched songs play from Jhapat's own timed upload; the rest are listed with covers, and Ask for these sends them to `request_songs`. Songs added to Jhapat later join the playlist by themselves when the catalog changes. Share copies the page's own address plus `#yt=<list id>` (jhapat.live or the github.io one, whichever it was opened on; v35), which reads YouTube again on whatever phone opens it. Report (v32, called Something off? until v35), under Next, sends what was wrong (word came too soon, came late, wrong word, not sung, video won't play, or a note) with the song, upload, word, its time, run-up and version to `report_drop`; without signal it waits in `S.outbox` for the next visit that reaches the database. The 16 songs built into `seed()` are only a fallback for phones that have never reached the database. Kept short on purpose: the video box stays folded until the first song plays, Start stands alone until then, a long item list shows its first 8 (plus any that are on or were tapped) and "+N more", run-up and video options sit in one folded Settings card, and playlist cards fan out their first songs' covers (`playlist_cards.thumbs`, YouTube's `mqdefault.jpg`).

- `studio/index.html`: the tagging studio, for editors only. It uses supabase-js 2.45.4 (UMD; verified live with the publishable key) and signs in by email link. Since v35 it wears the player's night look: party lights, the Dhaka band, the grid floor, and the logo bobbing. Good news (a toast saying added, saved, checked, fixed, synced and so on) gets a ding and a burst of emoji; the burst is off with reduced motion. It searches LRCLIB for timed lyrics, folds repeated lines together, highlights item words, and supports Hear/Tap/nudge per word with the timing copied to every repeat. A tap is refused unless it lands from 1 s before a repeat's line to 0.5 s after the next line starts. It also handles upload length checks (within 2 s of the lyrics → synced) and sync, items and playlists. Untimed lyrics get **Time the lines**: play the upload and tap as each line starts (Again for a line sung more often than it's written, Skip for one that isn't sung, Undo); the taps become timed lines, every item word is auto-tagged from them, repeated lines group themselves, the upload counts as synced and a draft goes live as auto. A draft whose timed lyrics come from another recording offers **Time the lines again** on its own upload: the old estimates make way for new ones, tags timed by hand stay, and other uploads need a new sync. Item-by-item tap-along stays for songs with no lyrics at all (folded away when there are words to time). Item words are coloured by state (dashed = not tagged, light gold = estimated, gold = timed or checked by ear); "Auto-tag them" estimates every untagged one, and Items › "Auto-tag all songs" does it everywhere after a new item or spelling. **Check tags** plays only the drop points, from 3 s before with a countdown, one stop per line (a chorus counts once). Drop points follow the player's rule, worked out for every mix of the song's items on the table (`dropSet`), so the red dots in the lyrics are exactly the words the game can drop into; a dot that only applies with another item out says so in its hover text ("when Maya is out too"). Right marks it checked, Earlier/Later moves every repeat. Every item word stays tagged, so the player knows where each word is and never starts a run-up across one, but only drop points are ever dropped into, so only they need hearing. The Tags card counts words and drop points per item, the song list and item tiles count drop points, and each song shows when it was added, changed, last tagged by hand and last checked by ear (`songs.tagged_at` and `checked_at`, kept by statement triggers on tags; the song list sorts by them). **Requests** (`#requests`, v31) lists songs people asked for from their YouTube playlists, most asked first, flagged "Maybe already in Jhapat" by the player's name rule; picked ones go into Add many as `Title - Artist | link` lines (the channel's own name dropped from the title, " - Topic" from the channel), and adding a song closes its request. **Reports** (`#reports`, v32) lists drops reported from the player, newest first; Open plays the reported word from 3 s before in Check tags, on the upload that played, with Mark the report fixed. Both tabs show a count of open ones. v39: Reports also gets the player's own reports and **Check every upload** (oEmbed for every upload of a public song, 8 at a time; 401/403 embedding off, 404 private or removed; Turn off sets `videos.embeddable = false`, which takes it out of the catalog). **Requests** since v40 opens with Waiting in people's playlists (`wishes`): grouped by Can add (timed lyrics), Needs timing by hand and Needs tagging by hand, most asked first, with Copy for Claude (the ones with timed lyrics as Add many lines with `| lrclib:` and length, for a session to find same-length Topic uploads), Add with uploads to Add many (ones from YouTube playlists), Added and Skip; one closes by itself once its LRCLIB version or upload is in the studio. The v31–v39 YouTube requests sit under it. **Stats** (`#stats`, v39) shows the counts: phones, new phones, nights started, parties, drops and shares for 7 or 30 days with the change on the period before, a bar a day (parties inside in red), and top playlists, songs (last 4 weeks), time zones, how new phones arrived, sharing and features. The marigold garland is gone from the studio too, and its version check now runs on jhapat.live. **Add many** (`#import`) takes `Title - Artist | YouTube link | m:ss` lines, measures each upload in a muted off-screen player, picks LRCLIB's version of the same length, auto-tags it and sets status `auto`; the rest come in as drafts. A line can end with `| lrclib:12345` to use that exact LRCLIB version (fetched by id, one request, no search). LRCLIB allows about 45 requests a minute from a browser and 90 from a script with its own User-Agent, per IP, so the studio spaces requests 1.4 s apart and a "too many requests" answer waits for its Retry-After and asks again; a row it never answers is marked and left out of Add, and "Find again" looks up only those rows. ## Data rules
- Tag times are on the lyrics' timeline. Each upload (`videos.offset_s`) stores how far it runs behind the lyrics, so video time = lyric time + offset. Never re-tag because an upload changed; fix that upload's offset.
- `songs.status`: draft (hidden) → auto (tagged automatically from lyrics the same length as the upload; public) → tagged (someone tagged or timed by hand) → checked. Tagging by hand moves draft or auto to tagged.
- `tags.rep` is the first line of the tag's repeated-line group (null for tap-along tags); the studio writes it with `line` and `word`.
- Item spellings (`items.words`) match at the start of a lyric word, in Devanagari and roman letters. A synonym the room would grab for counts (प्रेम and love for Maya); a kind of the thing doesn't (गुराँस isn't Phool), and neither does a name or another word that only starts the same (Phulmaya, चियाउनु): rule those out with a minus. Check a new spelling against every song's lyrics before adding it (see v41). A spelling starting with `-` rules words out for that item (`-दामी`, `-full`, `-रातो`). Don't add short roman spellings that are common words: roman `pani` was dropped from Paani because it's usually पनि (also). `items.emoji` shows on chips; `items.note` is the prop to put out.
- Lyrics live in the database and are fetched from LRCLIB at runtime. Don't copy lyrics into the repo, tests or docs. Test fixtures use invented lines.

- LRCLIB rate-limits by IP: keep scripts under about 75 requests a minute with an own User-Agent, honour Retry-After, and run one copy at a time (two copies once doubled the load).
